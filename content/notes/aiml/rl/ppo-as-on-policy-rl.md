---
title: "PPO as on-policy RL"
date: "2026-10-28"
---

PPO is classified as an **on-policy** algorithm.

### Why PPO is On-Policy

In reinforcement learning:

- **On-policy** methods learn the value or update the parameters of the exact policy currently generating the actions and trajectories.
- **Off-policy** methods evaluate or improve a target policy $\pi$ using data generated by a completely separate behavior policy $\beta$ (e.g., historical transitions stored in an arbitrary replay buffer, as in DQN, DDPG, or SAC).

PPO requires that the training transitions are generated by the policy currently being optimized, $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$. Once a rollout of data is collected and updated across a few gradient epochs, that rollout data becomes stale and must be discarded. PPO cannot learn from arbitrary historical replays or foreign exploration policies without degrading stability.

### Where Confusion Arises: Multi-Epoch Updates

PPO utilizes an **importance sampling ratio**:

$$
r_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t \mid s_t)}
$$

Because PPO runs multiple mini-batch epochs ($K \approx 3 \text{ to } 10$) on the same batch of rollouts, $\theta$ changes slightly with each gradient step. Technically, by epoch 2, the data was collected by $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$, while updates are applied to $\pi_\theta$.

However, this does not make PPO an off-policy algorithm in the traditional sense:

1. **Local Trust Region:** The clipping parameter $\epsilon$ strictly forces $\pi_\theta$ to stay in the immediate neighborhood of $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$. It cannot handle arbitrarily distant behavioral distributions.
2. **No Replay Buffer:** Rollouts are discarded immediately after the $K$ mini-batch passes. Data cannot be recycled across different iterations.
3. **Formal Categorization:** In RL theory, PPO is considered an on-policy method with a localized importance-sampling correction that allows multiple mini-batch updates on the current policy's rollout.
