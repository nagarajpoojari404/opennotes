Initializers

Weight initialization sets the starting values of trainable parameters to prevent gradients from vanishing or exploding during the forward and backward passes. In modern deep learning and Large Language Models (LLMs), initializers are derived by analyzing how variance propagates through linear, recurrent, or attention layers.

Variance Propagation Fundamentals

Consider a forward linear layer:

y=Wx=∑i=1ninwixi y = W x = \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{in}}} w_i x_i y=Wx=i=1∑nin​​wi​xi​

Assuming:

  • The inputs xix_ixi​ are independent and identically distributed with E[xi]=0\mathbb{E}[x_i] = 0E[xi​]=0 and Var(xi)=σx2\text{Var}(x_i) = \sigma_x^2Var(xi​)=σx2​.
  • The weights wiw_iwi​ are independent, drawn from a distribution with E[wi]=0\mathbb{E}[w_i] = 0E[wi​]=0 and Var(wi)=σw2\text{Var}(w_i) = \sigma_w^2Var(wi​)=σw2​.
  • wiw_iwi​ and xix_ixi​ are mutually independent.

The variance of yyy is:

Var(y)=∑i=1ninVar(wixi)=∑i=1nin(E[wi2xi2]−(E[wi]E[xi])2)=ninσw2σx2 \text{Var}(y) = \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{in}}} \text{Var}(w_i x_i) = \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{in}}} \left( \mathbb{E}[w_i^2 x_i^2] - (\mathbb{E}[w_i] \mathbb{E}[x_i])^2 \right) = n_{\text{in}} \sigma_w^2 \sigma_x^2 Var(y)=i=1∑nin​​Var(wi​xi​)=i=1∑nin​​(E[wi2​xi2​]−(E[wi​]E[xi​])2)=nin​σw2​σx2​

To maintain constant signal magnitude across depth (Var(y)≈Var(xi)\text{Var}(y) \approx \text{Var}(x_i)Var(y)≈Var(xi​)):

σw2=1nin \sigma_w^2 = \frac{1}{n_{\text{in}}} σw2​=nin​1​

During backpropagation, the gradient flow yields:

∂L∂xj=∑k=1noutwkj∂L∂yk  ⟹  Var(∂L∂xj)=noutσw2Var(∂L∂y) \frac{\partial L}{\partial x_j} = \sum_{k=1}^{n_{\text{out}}} w_{kj} \frac{\partial L}{\partial y_k} \implies \text{Var}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_j}\right) = n_{\text{out}} \sigma_w^2 \text{Var}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial y}\right) ∂xj​∂L​=k=1∑nout​​wkj​∂yk​∂L​⟹Var(∂xj​∂L​)=nout​σw2​Var(∂y∂L​)

Preserving gradient variance requires σw2=1nout\sigma_w^2 = \frac{1}{n_{\text{out}}}σw2​=nout​1​.

Classical Neural Network Initializers

1. Zero & Constant Initialization

  • Formula: W=0W = 0W=0 or W=cW = cW=c
  • Problem: If all weights in a hidden layer are initialized to the same value, every neuron in that layer computes identical outputs and receives identical gradients: ∂L∂wij=∂L∂yixj \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_{ij}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial y_i} x_j ∂wij​∂L​=∂yi​∂L​xj​ Symmetry is never broken, collapsing the layer to the representation capacity of a single unit.
  • Valid Use: Biases (b=0b = 0b=0) and normalization scaling parameters (γ=1,β=0\gamma = 1, \beta = 0γ=1,β=0).

2. Standard Normal & Uniform Initializers

  • Uniform: W∼U(−a,a)W \sim \mathcal{U}(-a, a)W∼U(−a,a) with σw2=a23\sigma_w^2 = \frac{a^2}{3}σw2​=3a2​.
  • Normal: W∼N(0,σ2)W \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)W∼N(0,σ2) where commonly σ=0.01\sigma = 0.01σ=0.01 or 0.020.020.02.
  • Limitation: Fixed variance independent of fan-in/fan-out causes exponential growth or decay across deep networks (LLL layers): Var(yL)≈(nσ2)LVar(x) \text{Var}(y_L) \approx (n \sigma^2)^L \text{Var}(x) Var(yL​)≈(nσ2)LVar(x)

3. LeCun Initialization

Designed for linear transformations and sigmoid-like activations in their near-linear regime:

  • Condition: Var(y)=Var(x)  ⟹  σw2=1nin\text{Var}(y) = \text{Var}(x) \implies \sigma_w^2 = \frac{1}{n_{\text{in}}}Var(y)=Var(x)⟹σw2​=nin​1​
  • Normal: W∼N(0,1nin) W \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, \frac{1}{n_{\text{in}}}\right) W∼N(0,nin​1​)
  • Uniform: Set a=3σw2=3nina = \sqrt{3 \sigma_w^2} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{n_{\text{in}}}}a=3σw2​​=nin​3​​: W∼U(−3nin,3nin) W \sim \mathcal{U}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{3}{n_{\text{in}}}}, \sqrt{\frac{3}{n_{\text{in}}}}\right) W∼U(−nin​3​​,nin​3​​)

4. Xavier / Glorot Initialization

Accounts for both forward activation variance and backward gradient variance by taking the harmonic mean of fan-in (ninn_{\text{in}}nin​) and fan-out (noutn_{\text{out}}nout​):

σw2=2nin+nout \sigma_w^2 = \frac{2}{n_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}}} σw2​=nin​+nout​2​
  • Normal: W∼N(0,2nin+nout) W \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, \frac{2}{n_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}}}\right) W∼N(0,nin​+nout​2​)
  • Uniform: W∼U(−6nin+nout,6nin+nout) W \sim \mathcal{U}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{6}{n_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}}}}, \sqrt{\frac{6}{n_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}}}}\right) W∼U(−nin​+nout​6​​,nin​+nout​6​​)
  • Best suited for: Symmetric, zero-centered activation functions (tanh⁡\tanhtanh, softsign, linear projections).

5. He / Kaiming Initialization

Designed for asymmetric non-linearities like ReLU (f(x)=max⁡(0,x)f(x) = \max(0, x)f(x)=max(0,x)). Because ReLU zeros out negative values, assuming zero-centered symmetric inputs yields:

E[f(y)2]=12E[y2]  ⟹  Var(f(y))≈12ninσw2Var(x) \mathbb{E}[f(y)^2] = \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}[y^2] \implies \text{Var}(f(y)) \approx \frac{1}{2} n_{\text{in}} \sigma_w^2 \text{Var}(x) E[f(y)2]=21​E[y2]⟹Var(f(y))≈21​nin​σw2​Var(x)

To counteract this 50% signal attenuation:

σw2=2nin \sigma_w^2 = \frac{2}{n_{\text{in}}} σw2​=nin​2​
  • Normal: W∼N(0,2nin) W \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, \frac{2}{n_{\text{in}}}\right) W∼N(0,nin​2​)
  • Uniform: W∼U(−6nin,6nin) W \sim \mathcal{U}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{6}{n_{\text{in}}}}, \sqrt{\frac{6}{n_{\text{in}}}}\right) W∼U(−nin​6​​,nin​6​​)
  • Leaky ReLU Extension: With negative slope parameter α\alphaα: σw2=2(1+α2)nin \sigma_w^2 = \frac{2}{(1 + \alpha^2) n_{\text{in}}} σw2​=(1+α2)nin​2​

6. Orthogonal Initialization

Addresses vanishing/exploding dynamics in deep linear and recurrent networks (RNNs/LSTMs) by initializing weight matrices as orthogonal transformations:

WTW=IorWWT=I W^T W = I \quad \text{or} \quad W W^T = I WTW=IorWWT=I

Every singular value σi(W)=1\sigma_i(W) = 1σi​(W)=1, preserving vector norms under repeated multiplications:

∥Wx∥2=∥x∥2 \Vert{}W x\Vert{}_2 = \Vert{}x\Vert{}_2 ∥Wx∥2​=∥x∥2​
  • Construction: Generate a random Gaussian matrix M∼N(0,1)M \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)M∼N(0,1), compute its QR decomposition M=QRM = QRM=QR, and assign W=Q⋅diag(sgn(diag(R)))W = Q \cdot \text{diag}(\text{sgn}(\text{diag}(R)))W=Q⋅diag(sgn(diag(R))).
  • Scaled Orthogonal: Often scaled by a gain factor ggg: W=g⋅Q(g=2 for ReLU) W = g \cdot Q \quad (g = \sqrt{2} \text{ for ReLU}) W=g⋅Q(g=2​ for ReLU)

Large Language Model (Transformer) Initializers

Deep decoder-only autoregressive models (GPT, LLaMA, Mistral, PaLM) introduce residual streams, attention scaling, and normalization layers that require specialized initialization schemes.

7. Truncated Normal Initialization

Standard Gaussian sampling occasionally produces extreme outliers in the tails (∣w∣>3σ\vert{}w\vert{} > 3\sigma∣w∣>3σ), causing early softmax saturation or training instability in low-precision regimes (FP16/BF16).

  • Formula: w∼N(0,σ2),constrained to w∈[−2σ,2σ] w \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2), \quad \text{constrained to } w \in [-2\sigma, 2\sigma] w∼N(0,σ2),constrained to w∈[−2σ,2σ] Values outside [−2σ,2σ][-2\sigma, 2\sigma][−2σ,2σ] are discarded and resampled until they fall within the band.
  • Common LLM Baseline: σ=0.02\sigma = 0.02σ=0.02 or σ=2dmodel+dffn\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{2}{d_{\text{model}} + d_{\text{ffn}}}}σ=dmodel​+dffn​2​​.

8. Residual-Scaled Initialization (GPT-2 / GPT-NeoX)

In models using standard Pre-LayerNorm or Post-LayerNorm architectures with residual connections:

xl+1=xl+fl(xl) x_{l+1} = x_l + f_l(x_l) xl+1​=xl​+fl​(xl​)

If each sub-layer flf_lfl​ outputs unit variance, the variance of the residual stream accumulates linearly with the number of layers NNN:

Var(xN)≈N⋅Var(x0) \text{Var}(x_N) \approx N \cdot \text{Var}(x_0) Var(xN​)≈N⋅Var(x0​)

To keep variance bounded at the output of the residual network, layers contributing to the residual branch (specifically the output projections: c_proj in MHA and down_proj in MLP) are scaled down by 12N\frac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}2N​1​:

  • Feedforward & Attention Input/Intermediates: W∼N(0,σ02),σ0=1dmodel W \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, \sigma_0^2\right), \quad \sigma_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{\text{model}}}} W∼N(0,σ02​),σ0​=dmodel​​1​
  • Residual Output Projections (c_proj, out_proj, down_proj): Wres∼N(0,σ022N)=N(0,12N⋅dmodel) W_{\text{res}} \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, \frac{\sigma_0^2}{2N}\right) = \mathcal{N}\left(0, \frac{1}{2N \cdot d_{\text{model}}}\right) Wres​∼N(0,2Nσ02​​)=N(0,2N⋅dmodel​1​) Here, NNN denotes the total number of Transformer blocks (accounting for two residual additions per block: multi-head self-attention and MLP).

9. DeepNorm Initialization

Introduced to stabilize very deep Transformers (e.g., 1000+ layers) without needing Post-LN warmup or failing to converge:

  • Residual formulation: xl+1=LayerNorm(xl⋅α+fl(xl,θl)) x_{l+1} = \text{LayerNorm}(x_l \cdot \alpha + f_l(x_l, \theta_l)) xl+1​=LayerNorm(xl​⋅α+fl​(xl​,θl​))
  • Variance scale condition: Bounds the expected activation magnitude: α=(2N)14 \alpha = (2N)^{\frac{1}{4}} α=(2N)41​
  • Parameter scaling: All weights in flf_lfl​ are scaled down by β\betaβ: W←β⋅W,β=(8N)−14 W \leftarrow \beta \cdot W, \quad \beta = (8N)^{-\frac{1}{4}} W←β⋅W,β=(8N)−41​ This ensures the variance growth per residual update stays below O(1/N)O(1/\sqrt{N})O(1/N​).
