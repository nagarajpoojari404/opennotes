Normalization

Normalization

Normalization techniques stabilize deep network training by controlling internal covariate shift, smoothing the loss landscape, and maintaining well-behaved gradient flows.

In tensor terminology, consider activations shaped as (N,C,H,W)(N, C, H, W)(N,C,H,W) for computer vision (Batch, Channel, Height, Width) or (B,T,D)(B, T, D)(B,T,D) for sequence models and LLMs (Batch, Sequence/Token, Hidden Dimension). The core difference across techniques is which dimensions are pooled to compute statistics.

1. Batch Normalization (BatchNorm)

BatchNorm computes statistics across the batch dimension and spatial/temporal positions for each individual channel or feature.

μc=1N×H×W∑n,h,wxn,c,h,w,σc2=1N×H×W∑n,h,w(xn,c,h,w−μc)2 \mu_c = \frac{1}{N \times H \times W} \sum_{n, h, w} x_{n, c, h, w}, \quad \sigma_c^2 = \frac{1}{N \times H \times W} \sum_{n, h, w} (x_{n, c, h, w} - \mu_c)^2 μc​=N×H×W1​n,h,w∑​xn,c,h,w​,σc2​=N×H×W1​n,h,w∑​(xn,c,h,w​−μc​)2x^=x−μcσc2+ϵ,y=γx^+β \hat{x} = \frac{x - \mu_c}{\sqrt{\sigma_c^2 + \epsilon}}, \quad y = \gamma \hat{x} + \beta x^=σc2​+ϵ​x−μc​​,y=γx^+β
  • Pooling axes: (N,H,W)(N, H, W)(N,H,W) or (B,T)(B, T)(B,T) per channel CCC or feature DDD.
  • Characteristics: Tracks running empirical means and variances during training to use as fixed constants during inference.
  • Why it fails in LLMs/RNNs:
    1. Sequence lengths vary across batches, causing padding artifacts in batch statistics. 2. Autoregressive inference generates one token at a time (B=1B=1B=1), where running statistics often mismatch generation dynamics. 3. Small per-device batch sizes in distributed LLM training introduce high variance into gradient updates.

2. Layer Normalization (LayerNorm)

LayerNorm computes statistics across the feature dimensions for each individual sample independently.

For a hidden state vector x∈RDx \in \mathbb{R}^Dx∈RD at batch item bbb and token position ttt:

μ=1D∑i=1Dxi,σ2=1D∑i=1D(xi−μ)2 \mu = \frac{1}{D} \sum_{i=1}^D x_i, \quad \sigma^2 = \frac{1}{D} \sum_{i=1}^D (x_i - \mu)^2 μ=D1​i=1∑D​xi​,σ2=D1​i=1∑D​(xi​−μ)2x^i=xi−μσ2+ϵ,yi=γix^i+βi \hat{x}_i = \frac{x_i - \mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}}, \quad y_i = \gamma_i \hat{x}_i + \beta_i x^i​=σ2+ϵ​xi​−μ​,yi​=γi​x^i​+βi​
  • Pooling axes: Feature/channel dimension DDD (per sample and per token position).
  • Characteristics: Zero batch dependency. Training and inference compute identical statistics on the fly.
  • LLM Role: The default normalization for original Transformers (Vaswani et al., BERT, GPT-2/GPT-3).

3. Root Mean Square Normalization (RMSNorm)

RMSNorm hypothesizes that the re-centering property (subtracting the mean μ\muμ) in LayerNorm is non-essential; the primary stabilizing effect comes from scaling by the magnitude.

RMS(x)=1D∑i=1Dxi2+ϵ \text{RMS}(x) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{D} \sum_{i=1}^D x_i^2 + \epsilon} RMS(x)=D1​i=1∑D​xi2​+ϵ​x^i=xiRMS(x),yi=γix^i \hat{x}_i = \frac{x_i}{\text{RMS}(x)}, \quad y_i = \gamma_i \hat{x}_i x^i​=RMS(x)xi​​,yi​=γi​x^i​
  • Characteristics: Removes mean computation μ\muμ and the learnable bias parameter β\betaβ.
  • Computational Benefit: Saves two passes over the hidden dimension vector in memory-bound GPU kernels (avoids mean accumulation and subtraction), improving kernel throughput by 10%–50% in standard fused operators.
  • LLM Role: The industry standard for modern open-weights and frontier LLMs (LLaMA 1/2/3, Mistral, Gemma, Qwen, DeepSeek).

4. Instance Normalization (InstanceNorm)

InstanceNorm normalizes across spatial dimensions (H,W)(H, W)(H,W) independently for each batch item and channel.

μn,c=1H×W∑h,wxn,c,h,w,σn,c2=1H×W∑h,w(xn,c,h,w−μn,c)2 \mu_{n,c} = \frac{1}{H \times W} \sum_{h, w} x_{n, c, h, w}, \quad \sigma_{n,c}^2 = \frac{1}{H \times W} \sum_{h, w} (x_{n, c, h, w} - \mu_{n,c})^2 μn,c​=H×W1​h,w∑​xn,c,h,w​,σn,c2​=H×W1​h,w∑​(xn,c,h,w​−μn,c​)2
  • Pooling axes: Spatial dimensions (H,W)(H, W)(H,W) per single (n,c)(n, c)(n,c) instance.
  • Characteristics: Strips contrast and global stylistic features from an image while preserving content structures.
  • Primary Use Case: Style transfer, generative image models (GANs), and visual domain adaptation. Rarely used in text/LLMs.

5. Group Normalization (GroupNorm)

GroupNorm divides the channel dimension CCC into GGG sequential groups (typically G=32G=32G=32) and computes the mean and variance across each group along with spatial dimensions.

μg=1(C/G)×H×W∑c∈Gk∑h,wxn,c,h,w \mu_g = \frac{1}{(C/G) \times H \times W} \sum_{c \in G_k} \sum_{h, w} x_{n, c, h, w} μg​=(C/G)×H×W1​c∈Gk​∑​h,w∑​xn,c,h,w​
  • Pooling axes: A slice of channels (C/G)(C/G)(C/G) and spatial dimensions (H,W)(H, W)(H,W) per sample.
  • Characteristics: Acts as a hybrid between LayerNorm (when G=1G=1G=1) and InstanceNorm (when G=CG=CG=C). It does not rely on batch size, making it a drop-in replacement for BatchNorm in computer vision tasks with small batch sizes (e.g., high-resolution object detection, 3D medical imaging, and Stable Diffusion U-Nets).

6. Weight Normalization & Spectral Normalization

These methods reparameterize the network’s weight matrices WWW rather than the activation vectors xxx.

Weight Normalization (WeightNorm)

Decouples the norm of the weight vector from its direction:

W=gV∥V∥2 W = g \frac{V}{\Vert{}V\Vert{}_2} W=g∥V∥2​V​
  • ggg is a scalar parameter controlling magnitude; VVV is a tensor parameterizing orientation.
  • Accelerates convergence in convolutional architectures and audio/waveform models (e.g., WaveNet).

Constrains the Lipschitz constant of linear/convolutional layers by dividing the weight matrix by its largest singular value (spectral norm σ(W)\sigma(W)σ(W)):

  • σ(W)\sigma(W)σ(W) is approximated via power iteration during the forward pass.
  • Prevents exploding gradients and mode collapse in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs).
