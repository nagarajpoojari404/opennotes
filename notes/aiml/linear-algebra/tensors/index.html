Tensors

At its core, a tensor is a geometric object that maps geometric entities (vectors and covectors) linearly to real numbers, retaining its physical identity regardless of the coordinate system chosen to describe it.

While computer science views tensors merely as multi-dimensional arrays, physics and mathematics define them by how their components transform under a change of basis.

1. Dual Spaces, Vectors, and Covectors

To understand tensors formally, we start on a vector space VVV of dimension nnn over R\mathbb{R}R:

  • Contravariant Vectors (Tangent Vectors, VVV): Elements of VVV. If {eμ}\{e_\mu\}{eμ​} forms a basis of VVV, a vector vvv is represented with an upper index: v=vμeμ v = v^\mu e_\mu v=vμeμ​
  • Covariant Vectors / 1-Forms / Covectors (V∗V^*V∗): Linear functions mapping V→RV \to \mathbb{R}V→R. If {θν}\{\theta^\nu\}{θν} is the dual basis satisfying θν(eμ)=δμν\theta^\nu(e_\mu) = \delta^\nu_\muθν(eμ​)=δμν​, a 1-form ω∈V∗\omega \in V^*ω∈V∗ is written with a lower index: ω=ωνθν \omega = \omega_\nu \theta^\nu ω=ων​θν

Under a coordinate transformation xμ→x′μx^\mu \to x'^\muxμ→x′μ:

  • Tangent vectors transform via the Jacobian matrix: v′μ=∂x′μ∂xαvα v'^\mu = \frac{\partial x'^\mu}{\partial x^\alpha} v^\alpha v′μ=∂xα∂x′μ​vα
  • Dual vectors (gradients of scalars, 1-forms) transform via the inverse Jacobian: ωμ′=∂xα∂x′μωα \omega'_\mu = \frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu} \omega_\alpha ωμ′​=∂x′μ∂xα​ωα​

2. General Tensor Definition

A tensor of type (r,s)(r, s)(r,s) (rank r+sr+sr+s) is a multilinear map:

T:V∗×⋯×V∗⏟r copies×V×⋯×V⏟s copies⟶R T: \underbrace{V^* \times \dots \times V^*}_{r \text{ copies}} \times \underbrace{V \times \dots \times V}_{s \text{ copies}} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R} T:r copiesV∗×⋯×V∗​​×s copiesV×⋯×V​​⟶R
  • rrr is the contravariant rank (number of upper indices).
  • sss is the covariant rank (number of lower indices).

Transformation Law

A tensor TTT of type (r,s)(r, s)(r,s) transforms under coordinate change x→x′x \to x'x→x′ as:

T′ν1…νsμ1…μr=(∂x′μ1∂xα1…∂x′μr∂xαr)(∂xβ1∂x′ν1…∂xβs∂x′νs)Tβ1…βsα1…αr {T'}^{\mu_1 \dots \mu_r}_{\nu_1 \dots \nu_s} = \left(\frac{\partial x'^{\mu_1}}{\partial x^{\alpha_1}} \dots \frac{\partial x'^{\mu_r}}{\partial x^{\alpha_r}}\right) \left(\frac{\partial x^{\beta_1}}{\partial x'^{\nu_1}} \dots \frac{\partial x^{\beta_s}}{\partial x'^{\nu_s}}\right) T^{\alpha_1 \dots \alpha_r}_{\beta_1 \dots \beta_s} T′ν1​…νs​μ1​…μr​​=(∂xα1​∂x′μ1​​…∂xαr​∂x′μr​​)(∂x′ν1​∂xβ1​​…∂x′νs​∂xβs​​)Tβ1​…βs​α1​…αr​​

This transformation rule is the operational litmus test: an array of numbers is a tensor if and only if its components transform strictly according to this multilinear law.

3. Essential Tensor Algebra

Einstein Summation Convention

Repeated upper and lower indices imply summation over the dimension range (111 to nnn or 000 to 333):

AμBμ≡∑μ=1nAμBμ A^\mu B_\mu \equiv \sum_{\mu=1}^n A^\mu B_\mu AμBμ​≡μ=1∑n​AμBμ​

Tensor Product (⊗\otimes⊗)

Combines a tensor of type (r,s)(r, s)(r,s) and (p,q)(p, q)(p,q) into a tensor of type (r+p,s+q)(r+p, s+q)(r+p,s+q):

(A⊗B)ν1…νsβ1…βqμ1…μrα1…αp=Aν1…νsμ1…μrBβ1…βqα1…αp (A \otimes B)^{\mu_1 \dots \mu_r \alpha_1 \dots \alpha_p}_{\nu_1 \dots \nu_s \beta_1 \dots \beta_q} = A^{\mu_1 \dots \mu_r}_{\nu_1 \dots \nu_s} B^{\alpha_1 \dots \alpha_p}_{\beta_1 \dots \beta_q} (A⊗B)ν1​…νs​β1​…βq​μ1​…μr​α1​…αp​​=Aν1​…νs​μ1​…μr​​Bβ1​…βq​α1​…αp​​

Contraction

Setting an upper index equal to a lower index sums over that coordinate, reducing the tensor’s rank by 2:

Cνμ→contractionCμμ=Tr⁡(C)(a scalar) C^\mu_{\nu} \xrightarrow{\text{contraction}} C^\mu_\mu = \operatorname{Tr}(C) \quad (\text{a scalar}) Cνμ​contraction​Cμμ​=Tr(C)(a scalar)

Metric Tensor (gμνg_{\mu\nu}gμν​)

A symmetric (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2) tensor that defines the inner product on the tangent space and sets the line element:

ds2=gμνdxμdxν ds^2 = g_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu dx^\nu ds2=gμν​dxμdxν

The metric is invertible (gμαgαν=δνμg^{\mu\alpha} g_{\alpha\nu} = \delta^\mu_\nugμαgαν​=δνμ​) and provides a natural isomorphism to raise and lower indices:

vμ=gμνvν,ωμ=gμνων v_\mu = g_{\mu\nu} v^\nu, \qquad \omega^\mu = g^{\mu\nu} \omega_\nu vμ​=gμν​vν,ωμ=gμνων​

4. Tensor Calculus on Curved Manifolds

On curved spaces (or even flat spaces in curvilinear coordinates), the partial derivative of a tensor component, ∂μTν≡∂Tν∂xμ\partial_\mu T^\nu \equiv \frac{\partial T^\nu}{\partial x^\mu}∂μ​Tν≡∂xμ∂Tν​, fails to transform as a tensor because the basis vectors themselves vary from point to point:

∂eα∂xμ≠0 \frac{\partial e_\alpha}{\partial x^\mu} \neq 0 ∂xμ∂eα​​=0

The Covariant Derivative (∇\nabla∇ or ;;;)

To construct a genuine directional derivative that transforms tensorially, we introduce an affine connection Γμνλ\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}Γμνλ​ (the Christoffel symbols):

For a vector vνv^\nuvν:

∇μvν=∂μvν+Γμλνvλ \nabla_\mu v^\nu = \partial_\mu v^\nu + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda} v^\lambda ∇μ​vν=∂μ​vν+Γμλν​vλ

For a 1-form ων\omega_\nuων​:

∇μων=∂μων−Γμνλωλ \nabla_\mu \omega_\nu = \partial_\mu \omega_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} \omega_\lambda ∇μ​ων​=∂μ​ων​−Γμνλ​ωλ​

For a general (r,s)(r, s)(r,s) tensor, add a +Γ+\Gamma+Γ term for every contravariant index and a −Γ-\Gamma−Γ term for every covariant index:

∇λTνμ=∂λTνμ+ΓλσμTνσ−ΓλνσTσμ \nabla_\lambda T^\mu_\nu = \partial_\lambda T^\mu_\nu + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\sigma} T^\sigma_\nu - \Gamma^\sigma_{\lambda\nu} T^\mu_\sigma ∇λ​Tνμ​=∂λ​Tνμ​+Γλσμ​Tνσ​−Γλνσ​Tσμ​

Christoffel Symbols of the Second Kind

Under the Levi-Civita connection (which is metric-compatible, ∇σgμν=0\nabla_\sigma g_{\mu\nu} = 0∇σ​gμν​=0, and torsion-free, Γμνλ=Γνμλ\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}Γμνλ​=Γνμλ​), the connection coefficients are uniquely determined by the metric:

Γμνλ=12gλσ(∂μgνσ+∂νgμσ−∂σgμν) \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} g^{\lambda\sigma} \left( \partial_\mu g_{\nu\sigma} + \partial_\nu g_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu} \right) Γμνλ​=21​gλσ(∂μ​gνσ​+∂ν​gμσ​−∂σ​gμν​)

Crucial Note: Γμνλ\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}Γμνλ​ is not a tensor. Its transformation law contains an inhomogeneous second-derivative term ∂2xα∂x′μ∂x′ν\frac{\partial^2 x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu \partial x'^\nu}∂x′μ∂x′ν∂2xα​, which precisely cancels the non-tensorial term produced by differentiating components.

5. Curvature Tensors

Because covariant derivatives do not generally commute on curved spaces, their commutator reveals the intrinsic curvature of the manifold.

Riemann Curvature Tensor

Acting with the commutator on a vector field:

[∇μ,∇ν]vρ=(∇μ∇ν−∇ν∇μ)vρ=R σμνρvσ [\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu] v^\rho = (\nabla_\mu \nabla_\nu - \nabla_\nu \nabla_\mu) v^\rho = R^\rho_{\ \sigma\mu\nu} v^\sigma [∇μ​,∇ν​]vρ=(∇μ​∇ν​−∇ν​∇μ​)vρ=R σμνρ​vσ

Expressed explicitly in terms of Christoffel symbols:

R σμνρ=∂μΓνσρ−∂νΓμσρ+ΓμλρΓνσλ−ΓνλρΓμσλ R^\rho_{\ \sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda} \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda} \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma} R σμνρ​=∂μ​Γνσρ​−∂ν​Γμσρ​+Γμλρ​Γνσλ​−Γνλρ​Γμσλ​

Key Contractions of the Riemann Tensor

  1. Ricci Tensor (RμνR_{\mu\nu}Rμν​): Contraction over the first and third indices: Rμν=R μλνλ R_{\mu\nu} = R^\lambda_{\ \mu\lambda\nu} Rμν​=R μλνλ​
  2. Ricci Scalar (RRR): Contraction with the inverse metric: R=gμνRμν R = g^{\mu\nu} R_{\mu\nu} R=gμνRμν​
  3. Einstein Tensor (GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​): Divergence-free tensor governing spacetime geometry in General Relativity (∇μGμν=0\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0∇μGμν​=0 via the Bianchi identities): Gμν=Rμν−12Rgμν G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} R g_{\mu\nu} Gμν​=Rμν​−21​Rgμν​
