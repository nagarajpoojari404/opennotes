Chinchilla derivation

Here is a clean, rigorous step-by-step mathematical derivation of why scaling laws take the form of power laws, and why the 1:1 Chinchilla ratio emerges theoretically.

Part 1: Why Loss Follows a Power Law (L∝N−αL \propto N^{-\alpha}L∝N−α)

The power-law relationship between parameters NNN and loss LLL can be derived directly from manifold geometry and kernel spectral theory.

1. The Geometric Cover Argument

Assume human language exists on a ddd-dimensional manifold (surface) embedded within a higher-dimensional vector space.

  1. Space Partitioning: A neural network with NNN parameters can partition this ddd-dimensional manifold into NNN distinct region cells (or “boxes”) to approximate the true language distribution.
  2. Cell Radius: The volume of the entire manifold is constant. Dividing it into NNN equal cells means each cell has a volume Vcell∝1NV_{\text{cell}} \propto \frac{1}{N}Vcell​∝N1​.
  3. Linear Characteristic Length (ϵ\epsilonϵ): Since volume in ddd dimensions scales as length to the ddd-th power (V∝ϵdV \propto \epsilon^dV∝ϵd), the average linear diameter ϵ\epsilonϵ of each cell is: ϵ∝(1N)1d=N−1d \epsilon \propto \left(\frac{1}{N}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}} = N^{-\frac{1}{d}} ϵ∝(N1​)d1​=N−d1​
  4. Approximation Loss: For smooth functions, the local error (loss) inside a cell of size ϵ\epsilonϵ scales quadratically or linearly with the cell size ϵ\epsilonϵ (via Taylor expansion): L(N)−L∞∝ϵβ L(N) - L_{\infty} \propto \epsilon^\beta L(N)−L∞​∝ϵβ where L∞L_{\infty}L∞​ is the irreducible entropy of language, and β≥1\beta \ge 1β≥1 depends on the smoothness of the model.
  5. The Power-Law Exponent: Substituting ϵ∝N−1/d\epsilon \propto N^{-1/d}ϵ∝N−1/d into the error equation gives: L(N)−L∞∝(N−1d)β=N−βd L(N) - L_{\infty} \propto \left(N^{-\frac{1}{d}}\right)^\beta = N^{-\frac{\beta}{d}} L(N)−L∞​∝(N−d1​)β=N−dβ​

Setting αN=βd\alpha_N = \frac{\beta}{d}αN​=dβ​ yields the empirical parameter scaling law:

L(N)−L∞=A⋅N−αN L(N) - L_{\infty} = A \cdot N^{-\alpha_N} L(N)−L∞​=A⋅N−αN​

Key Takeaway: The scaling exponent αN\alpha_NαN​ reflects the ratio of network smoothness (β\betaβ) to the intrinsic dimensionality (ddd) of natural language. Because text has a high intrinsic dimension ddd, αN\alpha_NαN​ is a small fraction (typically 0.050.050.05 to 0.340.340.34).

Part 2: Formulating the Joint Loss Function L(N,D)L(N, D)L(N,D)

When training with both finite parameters (NNN) and finite tokens (DDD), the model faces two independent sources of error:

  1. Representation Error (A⋅N−αA \cdot N^{-\alpha}A⋅N−α): Bottleneck from having too few parameters to store patterns.
  2. Estimation Error (B⋅D−βB \cdot D^{-\beta}B⋅D−β): Bottleneck from seeing too few data samples to estimate parameters accurately (variance from Stochastic Gradient Descent).

Assuming these two errors are additive alongside the irreducible loss (EEE), the joint loss equation formulated in the Chinchilla paper is:

L(N,D)=E+ANα+BDβ L(N, D) = E + \frac{A}{N^\alpha} + \frac{B}{D^\beta} L(N,D)=E+NαA​+DβB​
  • EEE: Irreducible loss (entropy of true natural language).
  • A,BA, BA,B: Constants related to data distribution scale.
  • α,β\alpha, \betaα,β: Empirical exponents governing parameter and data efficiency (α≈0.34\alpha \approx 0.34α≈0.34, β≈0.28\beta \approx 0.28β≈0.28).

Part 3: Deriving the 1:1 Compute-Optimal Scaling (Chinchilla)

To find how to allocate parameters NNN and dataset size DDD for a fixed compute budget CCC, we use constrained optimization.

1. The Compute Constraint

Training a Transformer model with NNN parameters on DDD tokens requires approximately 6 floating-point operations (FLOPs) per parameter per token (3 FLOPs for forward pass, 3 FLOPs for backward pass):

C=6ND C = 6ND C=6ND

2. Setting Up the Optimization Problem

We want to minimize the reducible loss subject to a fixed compute budget C0C_0C0​:

min⁡N,D(ANα+BDβ)subject to6ND=C0 \min_{N, D} \left( \frac{A}{N^\alpha} + \frac{B}{D^\beta} \right) \quad \text{subject to} \quad 6ND = C_0 N,Dmin​(NαA​+DβB​)subject to6ND=C0​

Substitute D=C06ND = \frac{C_0}{6N}D=6NC0​​ directly into the loss function to convert it into a single-variable function of NNN:

f(N)=AN−α+B(C06N)−β=AN−α+B⋅(6/C0)β⋅Nβ f(N) = A N^{-\alpha} + B \left( \frac{C_0}{6N} \right)^{-\beta} = A N^{-\alpha} + B \cdot (6/C_0)^\beta \cdot N^\beta f(N)=AN−α+B(6NC0​​)−β=AN−α+B⋅(6/C0​)β⋅Nβ

3. Taking the Derivative

To find the optimal parameter count N∗N^*N∗, set the derivative df(N)dN=0\frac{df(N)}{dN} = 0dNdf(N)​=0:

dfdN=−αAN−α−1+βB(6/C0)βNβ−1=0 \frac{df}{dN} = -\alpha A N^{-\alpha - 1} + \beta B (6/C_0)^\beta N^{\beta - 1} = 0 dNdf​=−αAN−α−1+βB(6/C0​)βNβ−1=0

Equating the two terms:

αAN−α−1=βB(6C0)βNβ−1 \alpha A N^{-\alpha - 1} = \beta B \left(\frac{6}{C_0}\right)^\beta N^{\beta - 1} αAN−α−1=βB(C0​6​)βNβ−1

4. Solving for Optimal N∗N^*N∗ and D∗D^*D∗ in Terms of Compute (C0C_0C0​)

Rearrange the terms to group NNN on one side:

Nβ−1N−α−1=Nα+β=αAβB⋅(C06)β \frac{N^{\beta - 1}}{N^{-\alpha - 1}} = N^{\alpha + \beta} = \frac{\alpha A}{\beta B} \cdot \left(\frac{C_0}{6}\right)^\beta N−α−1Nβ−1​=Nα+β=βBαA​⋅(6C0​​)β

Taking both sides to the power of 1α+β\frac{1}{\alpha + \beta}α+β1​:

N∗=(αAβB)1α+β(C06)βα+β N^* = \left( \frac{\alpha A}{\beta B} \right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha + \beta}} \left(\frac{C_0}{6}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha + \beta}} N∗=(βBαA​)α+β1​(6C0​​)α+ββ​

By symmetry, repeating this for D∗=C06N∗D^* = \frac{C_0}{6N^*}D∗=6N∗C0​​ yields:

D∗=(βBαA)1α+β(C06)αα+β D^* = \left( \frac{\beta B}{\alpha A} \right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha + \beta}} \left(\frac{C_0}{6}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha + \beta}} D∗=(αAβB​)α+β1​(6C0​​)α+βα​

Part 4: Plugging in Empirical Exponents (α≈β\alpha \approx \betaα≈β)

Hoffmann et al. (2022) measured the exponent values empirically across 400+ runs and found that α\alphaα and β\betaβ are remarkably close in value:

  • α≈0.34\alpha \approx 0.34α≈0.34
  • β≈0.28\beta \approx 0.28β≈0.28

Taking the balanced approximation where α≈β\alpha \approx \betaα≈β:

βα+β≈αα+β≈12=0.5 \frac{\beta}{\alpha + \beta} \approx \frac{\alpha}{\alpha + \beta} \approx \frac{1}{2} = 0.5 α+ββ​≈α+βα​≈21​=0.5

Substituting this back into our optimal N∗N^*N∗ and D∗D^*D∗ solutions:

N∗∝C00.5=C0 N^* \propto C_0^{0.5} = \sqrt{C_0} N∗∝C00.5​=C0​​D∗∝C00.5=C0 D^* \propto C_0^{0.5} = \sqrt{C_0} D∗∝C00.5​=C0​​

Conclusion

N∗D∗=Constant  ⟹  D∗∝N∗ \frac{N^*}{D^*} = \text{Constant} \implies D^* \propto N^* D∗N∗​=Constant⟹D∗∝N∗

Because both optimal parameters (N∗N^*N∗) and optimal tokens (D∗D^*D∗) scale proportional to C0\sqrt{C_0}C0​​, when compute CCC is scaled by a factor of kkk, both model parameters NNN and training tokens DDD must be scaled by k\sqrt{k}k​ (a 1:11:11:1 ratio).
